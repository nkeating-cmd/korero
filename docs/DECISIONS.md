# Kōrero: decision log

The one decision log for Kōrero: decisions that change how Kōrero is built, released or behaves, oldest first.
Started 16 Oct 2026. D-001 to D-006 are backfilled from the project record (`KORERO_STATE.md` and the build notes
in the Kōrero claude.ai project), where some carried older labels (shown in brackets); those labels are retired.

- **Status:** `Proposed` (written down, not decided; binds nothing), `Accepted`, `Superseded by D-NNN` or
  `Withdrawn`. The code follows `Accepted` rows only.
- **Adding a row:** it goes in the same PR as the change it records. Take the next free number on `korero/main`
  when the PR is ready to merge, not when drafting.
- **Product order** (what to build next) lives in `docs/PRODUCT.md`, not here.

| D-001 | 2026-09-25 | Accepted | **Kōrero is built and released from the fork** (`nkeating-cmd/korero`, branch `main`). The overlay-and-patches build in `korero-build-tooling` (`korero-build.ps1`, `korero-ship.ps1`) is not used for releases. (Was "D8".) | Measured 23 Sept: the overlay was at 1.38.0 and none of the 26 files v1.40 changed matched it; `korero-ship.ps1` would force-push `korero-fork` over `main`. 1.41.0 shipped from the fork. | Overlay plus patches on upstream Handy: keeps upstream close, but the landing debt was already larger than the features. | A rebase onto upstream Handy is attempted (191+ commits behind at the time). |
| D-002 | 2026-09-25 | Accepted | **New Zealand English is the default** (`default_selected_language = "en-NZ"`). A one-time step moves an inherited `en` to `en-NZ` and records that it ran, so a user who switches it off keeps their choice. (Was "D12"; finding F13.) | The NZ pass (macrons, NZ place names and spelling) only runs under `en-NZ`, and the default was `en`, so it had never run for anyone on the default, including both of Nic's machines. | Leave `en` and add a prompt: depends on users noticing a setting they did not know mattered. | A user reports the NZ pass changing text they did not want changed. |
| D-003 | 2026-10-02 | Accepted | **Calls to a local Ollama model use Ollama's own chat API with the context sized to the request**, not its OpenAI-compatible endpoint; thinking is off for Ollama (1.42). | Ollama's default context is 4k tokens on GPUs under 24 GB and the OpenAI endpoint cannot raise it, so long meetings got notes from part of the transcript, silently. gemma4:12b timed out with thinking on. | Tell users to raise the context in Ollama: invisible to most users and not checked by the app. | Ollama's OpenAI endpoint gains a context setting; another local runtime is added. |
| D-004 | 2026-10-02 | Accepted | **Before a local model reads a long transcript, the speech model is freed** (local model, at least 4,000 characters, nothing recording, unload not set to Never; never waits). Nic chose this ("option A") for 1.43. | Both models together did not fit the graphics card on the main laptop. Cost: Parakeet reloads in about 4 s, mostly hidden behind the next recording. | Keep both loaded: out-of-memory on 12 GB cards. Smaller notes model: worse notes. | A machine with ample video memory is the norm, or the reload cost becomes noticeable. |
| D-005 | 2026-10-08 | Accepted | **Notes for a meeting over 48,000 characters are written in parts and combined** (split at speaker turns, at most 8 parts); shorter meetings are unchanged. Nic: "go with reading in parts". | 8 of 35 meetings on the main laptop had notes from the start only, and lost every flagged moment. | A bigger context window: needs more video memory than the target machines have, and quality falls off on long inputs. | Ask needs whole-meeting answers (it still reads one part and says so). |
| D-006 | 2026-10-08 | Accepted | **`wasapi` was upgraded from 0.23 to 0.25 to clear a security advisory**, rather than recording an exemption. Nic's choice during the 1.44 publish gates. | An exemption leaves the advisory in place; the upgrade's side effect (Windows manifest embedding in test binaries) was fixable. | Exempt the advisory in `cargo deny`. | — |
| D-007 | 2026-10-16 | Accepted | **In-room speaker separation runs in Kōrero's own code on its existing ONNX runtime** (`ort 2.0.0-rc.12`), after Stop, on the CPU, never live: pyannote segmentation-3.0 (MIT) read for speaker changes, not only speech; WeSpeaker ResNet34-LM speaker embeddings (CC BY 4.0, attribution in the third-party notices); clustering of the whole meeting on segments of 1.5 s or more, clusters under 20 s of speech folded into the nearest one, short fragments attached by voice. Plan: `KORERO_1.45_IN-ROOM-SPEAKERS-PLAN_2026-10-16.md` (v2). (Approved by Nic 16 Oct 2026: "Execute D007".) | M0 probe, 16 Oct, on four AMI test meetings recorded by one far-field room microphone (92 minutes): mean error 23.1% with a 0.25 s collar, 28.1% strict, against 22.4% strict for pyannote's own pipeline on all 16 test meetings; right number of people in 2 of 4 meetings (one too many in one, two too few in the other). About 80 s of CPU time per hour of audio on 4 threads. It builds in the same binary as `transcribe-rs` with one `ort`. | `pyannote-rs` 0.3.4: does not compile against `ort` rc.12 (29 errors); reads the model only as a speech detector; its clock drifts 60.6 ms per 10 s; its packaged CAM++ embedding model told AMI speakers apart no better than chance, even on headset audio (57.5% error with the right model, 80.5% as published). `sherpa-onnx`: a second ONNX runtime beside `ort`. Telling the clusterer the number of people: worse (31.5%) than finding it. The notes model guessing speakers: invents attributions. | Results on Nic's own room recordings differ from AMI; `knf-rs` (C++ build, `ndarray` 0.16) is replaced by Rust feature code. |
| D-008 | 2026-10-16 | Accepted | **The speaker-separation models are downloaded on first use, not bundled.** "Separate speakers" fetches pyannote segmentation-3.0 (5,983,836 B) and WeSpeaker ResNet34-LM (26,530,550 B) into the models folder (`diarise/`), writes each as `.partial`, and keeps it only if its size and sha256 match the values pinned in `diarise.rs`; a mismatch is deleted and never loaded. Sources for now: the `pyannote-rs` v0.1.0 and sherpa-onnx speaker-recognition-models GitHub releases. Filterbank features come from `knf-rs` 0.3.2 (MIT; a C++ build through CMake and bindgen, and a second `ndarray`, 0.16, internal to it). | Bundling would roughly triple the installer (14 MB to about 46 MB) for every user, including those who never record in a room. Download-on-first-use matches how Kōrero already fetches speech models and stays offline afterwards. Pinning size and hash means a changed or substituted file at either URL fails closed. | Bundle in the installer: simplest offline story, but the size cost falls on everyone. Mirror to Kōrero's own GitHub release now: more control, but publishing a release is Nic's call. Rust filterbanks instead of `knf-rs`: removes the C++ build, but needs a parity check first (plan M2 step c). | Either upstream URL changes or disappears (the pinned hash makes that a clean failure, not a silent one): mirror both files to a Kōrero release. The Rust filterbank parity check passes (drop `knf-rs`). |
| D-009 | 2026-10-17 | Accepted | **Meetings are chosen as "On a call" or "In the room" before Record** (remembered on the computer). In the room: the microphone only is recorded (no system capture, so no call-audio warnings); the live transcript is a preview with a lower energy floor (0.003, Silero still deciding what is speech); and after Stop the saved transcript is rebuilt from the speaker model's turns, one line per turn with its speaker and true start time (`diarise::meeting_room_transcript`). If that fails or finds nothing, the live lines are kept. (Nic, 17 Oct: "there should be the ability to select if it is a in-person meeting up front, so it knows there is nothing on the call".) | On four AMI room-microphone meetings, the fixed 0.010 floor passed 2-9% of labelled speech in three (median speech frame RMS 0.0024-0.0029; 57% in the fourth, a louder room), so live capture and the energy-based rebuild at Stop both lose most far-field speech. The segmentation model misses only 7-15% there (M0). Transcribing per turn also removes the estimated line ends and the mid-sentence speaker changes of D-007's labelling. | A lower fixed floor everywhere: rooms vary five-fold in level, and calls would pass more noise. An adaptive noise-floor gate: more moving parts and still energy-based; worth it later for calls. Asking the user for the number of people: made separation worse in M0. | Results on Nic's own rooms; overlapping speech (transcribed once, for whoever was already speaking); Hybrid meetings (B8). |
| D-010 | 2026-10-23 | Accepted | **A local Ollama dictation clean-up is never cancelled for running late.** The person at the cursor waits at most 30 s (`actions::CURSOR_WAIT`, as before). If the clean-up is not back by then, the words are pasted as spoken and the pill says "Pasted as spoken · clean-up continues". The clean-up carries on as its own task (up to `ollama_chat::DICTATION_LIMIT`, 300 s) and is filed on that dictation's History entry only while the entry still holds the same words and has no clean-up. Other providers (cloud, `custom`) keep their own 30 s client limit and are waited for as before, because a late result could never arrive (`can_finish_late`). History retry and Notes wait for the answer. When a clean-up dictation starts and the Ollama model is **not loaded at all**, Kōrero loads it (`prewarm_cleanup_model` → `warm_if_cold`; no transcript text sent) at a context with room for about 4,000 characters of dictation (`PREWARM_TRANSCRIPT_CHARS`, 6,144 tokens with the default prompt), so the request reuses that load. A pre-warm may run 300 s (was 120 s). Bug fix; Nic, 23 Oct: "make sure that what you fix is the best fix that's as persistent as possible". | Two clean-ups on 23 Oct on the main laptop failed at 30.1 s with the model still loading (Ollama log 13:47 and 14:17; Kōrero log: `loaded=None`, "didn't finish its answer within 30 s"). Cold loads of gemma4:12b took 14-19 s on 19-20 Oct and 108 s on 23 Oct with about 3 GB of RAM free, so no fixed wait at the cursor covers every load. Probes on 23 Oct: when the request waiting on a load disconnects, Ollama aborts the load even if another request is waiting (B), so each give-up threw the half-loaded model away; a request sent while a pre-warm is loading at the same context joins that load (C: one load, answered 17.6 s after sending). Live test, cold model: answered 35.0 s after sending, one load, no abort. Costs accepted, measured or bounded: loading the model while recording slowed transcribing a 30 s clip by 0.2-0.3 s (2.55-2.86 s alone, 2.77-3.17 s with a cold load running; two runs each, 3 GB RAM free); a cancelled recording leaves the model loaded for its 30 min keep-alive; late clean-ups are not capped and each can hold an Ollama request for up to 300 s; a dictation over about 4,000 characters still reloads the model at a bigger context. An independent review found three Major issues, fixed before merge: the late path and its pill text applied to providers that can never finish late; a pre-warm sized for the prompt alone made long dictations load the model twice; the overlap with transcription was unmeasured. | Raise the 30 s limit to cover the slowest load: a paste arriving two minutes after Stop lands in whatever window has focus by then, and 108 s may not be the worst. Keep the model loaded all day (`keep_alive` -1): holds about 7.5 GB of graphics memory whether or not anyone dictates, against D-004. Warn only: tells the person, but the clean-up is still lost and the load still thrown away. Skip the pre-warm when memory is low: the measured cost was small, and the low-memory case is where the pre-warm saves most. | Ollama stops aborting a shared load when one waiter leaves; another local runtime is added (`custom` has neither the late path nor a pre-warm); late clean-ups pile up in practice; people find the late History update confusing. |
